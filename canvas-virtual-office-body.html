Getting help, and giving it

Dr. Rennie's Virtual Office

This board is open all term and it is the front door for course questions. It is public on purpose. In a fully online class the hallway conversation after lecture does not happen by accident, so we hold it here, where everyone can benefit from it.

To ask something, hit Reply below. To answer a classmate, reply to their post.

Why your question here helps everyone

If you are confused about something, you are almost certainly not the only one. You are just the one willing to say it out loud. A question asked here gets answered once and read by the whole class, and the best ones get folded into the answered questions page, where they keep helping students for terms to come. Asking a good question in public is a contribution to this class, and I treat it as one.

Which door for which question

Three places to bring something, and they do different jobs. Picking the right one gets you a useful answer faster.

Public board

Here, the Virtual Office

Anything about the course that is not private: content you are stuck on, what an assignment is asking for, where something lives, a page that will not load. A thread here often collects two or three ways into the same problem, which is worth more than any one of them on its own.

Course content goes on the board, because working a problem through with other people, and seeing how someone else arrived at it, builds the reasoning you are graded on and sticks in a way a handed-over answer does not.

Private, in writing

The same private matters, in writing, any time. Grades, accommodations, personal circumstances, an emergency, or anything carrying your name and details. Private things never go on the public board. Send those to me directly.

How to post

Four lines, every post

Every post on this board answers these four questions. This is not red tape. Writing them out is usually the fastest way through the problem, and more often than you would expect you will answer your own question somewhere around the third line.

  1. What am I trying to figure out?
    The actual question, in one sentence. "How does this work" is too broad to answer. "Why does the pressure drop when the vessel widens" can be answered in a paragraph.
  2. Where did I already look?
    Name the page, the section of the reading, or the video and roughly where in it. This stops three people sending you back to something you have already read.
  3. What did I try?
    Your reasoning so far, even the part you are not sure about. Especially the part you are not sure about, because that is usually where the answer is hiding.
  4. Where did it stop making sense?
    The exact sentence, step, or number where you lost the thread. This is the single most useful line in the post, and it is the one that gets you a fast answer instead of a lecture.

Worked it out while typing it? Post it anyway, and add the line that fixed it. Those are some of the most useful posts on this board, they take you ten more seconds, and the next person who gets stuck in the same place will find it.

Three habits that keep the board fast

  • Give it a real title. "Confused about the second color on the note sheet" gets found and answered. "Help" gets neither.
  • One question per post. Three questions bundled together tend to get one and a half answers. Split them and each one gets a real reply.
  • Come back and close the loop. If a reply solved it, say so. The next student with your question needs to know which answer worked.

Answering each other

Answering each other is not a courtesy, it is the point. Explaining something you have just learned is one of the strongest study moves there is, and a concept you can walk someone else through is a concept you own. Work the problem with each other first, out loud or in writing, and post where you got stuck. I read the board, so anything that needs correcting gets corrected.

Reaching the answer yourselves is what makes it stick, and it is the skill the exams are built to reward, since every one of them asks you to reason out loud rather than pick a letter.
