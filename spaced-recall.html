What the tool is doing

Every question is tied to one course competency and sits at one of three levels. Level 1 asks you to recall something. Level 2 asks you to apply it. Level 3 asks you to work out what happens when conditions change. You start at level 1 on everything. Level 2 opens on a topic once you have proved level 1 on it, and level 3 opens the same way off level 2. You cannot skip ahead, and you do not have to unlock anything by hand.

When you get a card right it goes away for longer: one day, then four, then twelve, then thirty, then seventy-five. When you get it wrong it comes back tomorrow. A card counts as proved once you have answered it right on two separate days, which is why one long session cannot substitute for showing up on several days.

What covered means

A competency is covered when three of its cards are proved at every level it has cards for. That is the same bar the tool uses to open the next level, so covering a competency and unlocking its hardest questions are the same thing. There are 268 competencies in the course.

Covered does not mean every card in the bank. There are 4,980 cards and about eighteen per competency. The spares are there so there is always a fresh question waiting when you miss one.

What it takes

  • Keep up. About 15 cards a day, five days a week, roughly 8 to 10 minutes. You will stay current with the week being taught and hold on to what you have already learned. You will not cover the course this way, and that is a fair choice as long as you know you are making it.
  • Cover the course. Around 60 cards a day, seven days a week, roughly 25 minutes. Over a term that gets almost every competency in the course proved at level 1.
  • Going deeper than that. Getting every competency proved at level 2 as well takes about 125 cards a day, seven days a week, close to an hour. That is a real commitment on top of the reading, the note sheets and the lab, so think about whether the hour is better spent here or on the note sheets.

Days matter more than length

A card can only move forward once a day, no matter how many times you see it. Sixty cards on each of seven days moves you much further than four hundred cards on two days. If you have twenty minutes and a choice between doing them all today or ten minutes today and ten tomorrow, split them.

You do not have to pick chapters

The set is built for you every day. Cards that are due come first, oldest first, from any week of the course. Then a few cards you have never seen are added, with some always kept for the week being taught now and the rest taken from the oldest material you have not finished. So earlier chapters come back on their own, and nothing you started in week 3 gets stranded because week 9 arrived. There is no chapter menu because you do not need one.

What it cannot do for you

  • Material taught in the last two or three weeks of the term will not have time to climb the whole ladder before the term ends. For those weeks, lean on the note sheets and the teach-back work.
  • These cards are multiple choice. Every graded thing in this course asks you to draw a mechanism, teach it out loud, or answer out loud. The cards are practice for the thinking, not practice for the format.
