Why practice like this, and why more of it is better

Every exam you build is a different exam. The bank holds about 5,600 questions across the course, so you are not memorizing a fixed set. Sit the same week twice and you get two genuinely different exams, which means the second one measures whether you learned the physiology rather than whether you remembered the questions.

You get a breakdown, not just a score. At the end it tells you which competencies you are solid on, which are shaky, and which are gaps, and it separates the questions you got wrong while feeling sure (a misconception, the expensive kind) from the ones you got wrong while unsure (just a gap). Those two need completely different fixes, and knowing which one you have is most of the work.

One exam cannot cover everything. A 30 question exam samples the week; it does not exhaust it. So a competency you have never been asked about is not a competency you have proved. Every extra exam pulls a different sample and surfaces things the earlier ones never touched, which is the real reason repeated practice beats one careful attempt. The gaps you have not found yet are the ones that cost you on a midterm.

Practice is free here. It carries no points, you can run it as many times as you want, and nothing that happens in it counts against you.

Why I am asking for this

I want to reach the people who need help before a midterm rather than after one, and by the time a low exam grade tells me, it is too late to be useful to you.

Your practice log tells me two things a gradebook cannot. Whether you are practicing at all, and whether what you are doing is working. If your numbers are climbing, you are on a good path and I will leave you to it. If they are flat or falling, that is not a character problem, it is a method problem, and method problems are fixable in a week. That is when I want to be emailing you.

The scores are not graded, and that is deliberate

Nobody is graded on these numbers. Not the scores, not the average, not the trend. The upload is checked for existing and nothing else.

Here is the reasoning, because it matters. The student whose practice scores are dropping is exactly the student I most need to see an honest log from. If those numbers counted for points, that student would have every reason to run the generator until it looked good, or to stop submitting. Grading it would destroy the only thing it is for. So it carries no points, and in exchange I am asking you to let it be true.

What to do

  1. Practice during the week. Run a one week exam, a multi week Gap Finder, or a cumulative review, as often as you want. There is no minimum and there is no maximum.
  2. Press "Print my weekly practice log" on the practice exam page. Everything you ran is already in it: your attempts, your scores by week, and the competencies that would lift you the most.
  3. Choose Save as PDF as the print destination.
  4. Attach the PDF here by Sunday at 10:00 pm.

A week where you did nothing

Upload the log anyway, empty, and write one line in the comment box saying what got in the way. A week happens to everybody.

An empty log with a sentence attached tells me something useful and costs you nothing. A missing upload tells me nothing at all, and the thing I am most likely to do about it is assume the worst and email you about it, which wastes your time and mine.

Things worth knowing

  • The log lives in one browser on one device. If you practice on a laptop and a phone you will end up with two partial logs. The practice exam page has an export and import so you can move your history between them.
  • Clearing your browsing data clears the log. If that is coming, download a backup first.
  • Nothing here is sent anywhere. The log sits in your own browser until you print it and upload it yourself.
