Welcome to Human Physiology

Anatomy asks what the parts are. Physiology asks how they work, and what happens when they stop. Over fifteen weeks you will build one idea and then use it everywhere: your body is constantly being pushed off balance, and almost everything it does is the work of pushing back.

Part 1 · Weeks 1 to 3

How cells hold the line

Homeostasis, feedback, and the chemistry that does work. How things cross a membrane, and why that costs energy.

Part 2 · Weeks 4 to 8

Signals, movement and control

The electrical signal, synapses, the senses, how muscle makes force, and the hormones that run the slower half of control, including reproduction.

Part 3 · Weeks 9 to 15

The systems that keep you alive

The heart and circulation, blood and immune defense, digestion, breathing, the kidney, and how the body defends its pH from both ends.

How I teach this

This course is fully online, and it is built to be rigorous and flexible at the same time. Those two things are usually treated as a trade. They are not.

The flexibility is real. Nothing meets at a set time. You choose how you learn each week: watch it, read it, draw it, work problems, or say it out loud to an empty room. You choose which application case you solve, so the physiology lands in the room you are actually heading for, whether that is nursing, medicine, radiology, or something else. You choose what to change when something is not working. I have no interest in dictating how you learn, because you know things about your own life and your own head that I do not.

The rigor is also real. What you have to be able to do at the end is the same for everyone, and it is not recognizing an answer on a list. It is retrieving what you know with nothing open and using it on a problem you have not seen. You will be asked to prove that, out loud, on video, by drawing a mechanism and teaching it to me.

So: choice in how you learn, one common standard for what you can do. Every week gives you a Mastery Check that carries no points and tells you honestly whether the way you chose actually worked, while there is still time to change it.
