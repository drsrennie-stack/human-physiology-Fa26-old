A straight answer

How AI is used in this course.

I have been teaching physiology for ten years. I have spent thousands of hours writing and rewriting these lectures, designing and redesigning how each idea gets taught, and finding out, class by class, what actually makes it stick. The content of this course, what we cover, in what order, how it is explained, what gets asked of you and why, is mine.

AI did not write this course. I wrote this course. I use AI as a builder uses power tools: to organize my materials, to build the learning tools I designed but am not trained to code, the spaced recall cards, the drawing canvas, the practice systems, and to make the course visually clean so nothing gets between you and the physiology. I am in these pages continuously, monitoring and modifying them, and everything you read passed through my hands.

I expect the same honesty from you that I am giving you here. So the rules below are not about hiding from AI. They are about making sure the learning happens in your head, where the midterm and your future patients will look for it.

Where you can use AI, and how

Use it for these, freely

  • Checking your work, after your honest attempt. Solve the problem by hand first, write down your sources, then open AI to check your answer. The AI worksheet that travels with every problem set exists exactly for this, and it is part of the assignment.
  • Getting a concept explained a different way. If my explanation and the book's both miss for you, ask AI to explain it another way, then close the tab and re-explain it from memory in your own words. If you cannot, you do not have it yet.
  • Quizzing yourself. Ask it for practice questions on this week's competencies and answer them cold, out loud or on paper.
  • Planning your week. Fitting the course hours around work and family is a fine job for AI.
  • Hootie and the course tools. The course's own helpers were built for you; use them as much as you like.

Never for these

  • Writing or answering your note sheet. The sheet is your thinking in your two colors. An AI-written answer in your handwriting is still an AI-written answer, and it leaves you with nothing to retrieve on the midterm.
  • Doing the book problems before your attempt. AI comes after your handwritten work, never before it, and never instead of it. The worksheet documents the order.
  • Producing your lab work. Your data records, your plots, your predictions, and your look backs are yours, by hand.
  • Writing your discussion posts or replies. Your classmates deserve you, not a chatbot doing an impression of you.
  • Anything on the midterms. The exams are draw and teach, on video, from memory, with nothing open. That is the whole point of them.
  • Drawing your drawings. Every drawing in this course is graded because your hand made it while your brain built it. Generated or traced images earn a zero.
The one rule that covers every case: if AI touched the work before your brain and your hand did, it is not your work. If it checked, explained, or quizzed after your honest attempt, and you logged it where a log is asked for, you used it the way a professional does.
