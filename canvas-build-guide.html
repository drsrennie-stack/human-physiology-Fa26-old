1 · The analysis

What the course is, from Canvas's point of view

BIO 005 is fully online, 15 weeks, three parts (Foundations weeks 1 to 3, Control systems 4 to 8, Systems in action 9 to 15), one fixed weekly container, five graded categories (note sheets 20, midterms 40, lab 15, book problems 15, discussions 10), two midterm windows that sit at the front of weeks 8 and 15, and one due time all term: Sunday 10:00 pm, with discussion posts Friday 10:00 pm. Weeks open on their Monday and unlock the Saturday before at 8:00 pm Pacific. That is the whole shape, and Canvas can carry every piece of it natively: assignment groups carry the weights, modules carry the weeks, module lock dates carry the Saturday unlock, and the two midterms are ordinary assignments with an availability window.

The site already gives Canvas everything it needs and asks nothing back. The four assignment instruction pages read the week from the URL (?week=N), the week hubs exist for all fifteen weeks, the printable note sheet and the competency list are generated per week from the competency file, and the Mastery OS opens to a week. So the rule that keeps this build from breaking again is simple: Canvas holds the order, the dates and the grades. The site holds the content. Every Canvas item points at a URL that never changes. When a video or a notes page is ready, you edit the repo. You never open Canvas to fix content, and Canvas never has to know a page was late.

Two things were missing for that rule to hold. A per-week lecture page did not exist (Week 2's hub links to a notes file that is not in the repo, and weeks 3 to 15 have nowhere to point), so lecture-week.html is included: one file, a manifest at the top, and every Week module's Step 2 page frames it. And Canvas modules cannot nest, so the part structure, the step order and the predictability you asked for all have to come from naming and text headers, which is what the next section lays out.

The order students will learn once

Every week module is one Start Here page and six numbered steps, in this order, with a text header naming each step so the Modules page reads like a checklist. The week page on the site carries the identical six numbers, so a student sees the same list whether they are in Canvas or on the site. It is the order you described: sheet, then video, then the sheet again, then the discussion by Friday, then the lab, then the problems and the cards, then turn in the sheet and rehearse.

How the order is enforced. Each week module has requirements on the three pages (view) and on the three graded items (submit) and on the discussion (contribute), with the "students must move through requirements in sequential order" box ticked. That means a student cannot open the lectures before they have opened the note sheet page, and cannot see the discussion before the lectures page. It also means a student who never posts to the discussion cannot open the lab. If that second part is more gate than you want, take the submit requirements off and leave only the three view requirements: the reading order is still enforced, and no graded item is ever locked behind another graded item. Both are one checkbox each; the guide is written for the full version.
