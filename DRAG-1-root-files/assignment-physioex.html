What this is

Different lab this week

There is no PhysioEx this week. PhysioEx does not have an exercise that fits this material. Your lab for Week 1 is listed in the week page instead.

The sheet and the deadline do not change. Everywhere below that says "activity," read it as one part of that lab.

One sheet a week, covering everything you ran. If Week 1 has six PhysioEx activities, you do not write six reports. You write one, and it accounts for all six together.

PhysioEx is where you generate the data. This sheet is where you say what the week's set of experiments, taken together, actually showed you. It is handwritten, and it is an analysis rather than a data dump.

Due Sunday at 10:00 pm, the Sunday that closes Week 1. Up to 24 hours late is an automatic 50 percent reduction. After 24 hours it is a zero.

Why this sheet is built this way

Read this part

Let me be straight with you. PhysioEx is a simulation. The results are fixed, every student who runs it gets the same numbers, and the answers have been posted online for years. I know that. You know that.

So this sheet does not ask you to report the numbers back to me, because copying numbers proves nothing about whether you understood anything. Anyone can do that in four minutes without opening the program.

What it asks for instead is the part nobody has written up for you: what the week's activities showed when you put them next to each other, which one changed your mind, and what you would predict next. That is genuinely yours, it is in your handwriting, and there is no version of it sitting on a website waiting to be found.

You can absolutely use AI to check whether your reasoning holds together. Use it to argue with. What it cannot do is tell you which activity surprised you, or what you thought before you ran it.

Do it in this order

  1. Write down the week's activity names firstList every PhysioEx activity assigned for Week 1 on your sheet before you start. That list keeps you from losing track when a week has six of them.
  2. Open an activity and read its setup screenEvery PhysioEx activity starts by telling you what it is going to change and what it is going to measure. Read that part properly instead of clicking past it. It is the only thing you need in order to make a real prediction.
  3. Now write what you think will happen, and whyTwo or three sentences, before you press start. You have already done the reading and the videos this week, so you know the mechanism. This is asking what that mechanism predicts when someone changes the thing this activity is changing. Do it for one activity, whichever one you were least sure about. You are not graded on being right.
  4. Run them all, taking short notes as you goFor each activity, note what was manipulated, what was measured, and what happened in one line. Short. This is the raw material, not the report.
  5. Look for what they have in commonRead your rows next to each other. Is the body controlling the same thing in all of them? This is the real work of the assignment, and it is the part you cannot do while you are still clicking through the program.
  6. Say what surprised youWhich result was not what you expected, and what had you assumed that turned out to be wrong? If nothing surprised you, say so, and say what that tells you about how well you already knew this material.
  7. Show one cause and effect, step by stepStart with something that changed, then follow it in arrows all the way to what you measured. Use at least two of the activities you listed. This is where a list of tasks turns into physiology.
  8. Write what you know now that you did not know on MondayThree or four sentences in your own words. What can you say about how the body handles this, now that you have run all of these?
