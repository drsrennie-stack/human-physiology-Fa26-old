P-01

What Is Physiology?

slides-P-what-is-physiology.html · 6 slides

Before you start This is the first deck of the course. Spend a minute on why the definition matters before you open anything, because students think they already know what physiology is.

Slide 2 of 6

Start with the definition

Boxes on this slide What physiology is · What the goal is · What that means for us

Open with
Ask them to define physiology in their own words before you show anything.
As the boxes open
Open the definition first and let it sit. The word doing the work is normal. Physiology is the study of the working body, and pathophysiology is the same study when the working has broken. Open the goal next and point out that physical and chemical is a promise: no step in this course will be hand waved. Close on the third box and say that this is what separates a living body from the same atoms in a jar.
Do this
Have them write their own one-sentence definition, then rewrite it after the third box.
Slide 3 of 6

Anatomy tells you what is there. Physiology tells you what it does

Boxes on this slide The everyday version · Why you need both · Where this course lives

Open with
Say the anatomy and physiology comparison out loud before opening.
As the boxes open
Parts list and operating manual lands immediately, so let it. The second box is the one that matters for this course: they will be asked to explain, not just name. On the third box, say plainly that almost every exam question this term is a version of how does the body keep this working, and that if they can answer that they can answer most of what you ask.
Do this
Ask who has already had anatomy. Tell the ones who have not that they are not behind, because you teach the structure they need where they need it.
Slide 4 of 6

The themes that come back every single unit, part one

Boxes on this slide Homeostasis and control · Structure and function · Molecular interactions · Compartmentation

Open with
Tell them these four come back every single week, and that they should recognize them by Week 3.
As the boxes open
Homeostasis and control is the spine of the whole course. Structure and function is where anatomy pays off. Molecular interactions is the level everything reduces to. Compartmentation is the one they always underestimate, so linger there: the reason a cell can do anything at all is that it keeps things apart.
Do this
Name a system they already know and ask which theme it belongs to. Most answers will be two or three at once, which is the point.
Slide 5 of 6

The themes, part two

Boxes on this slide Biological energy transformation · Communication and information flow · A memory hook

Open with
Say that these last two are the ones students skip and then need in Week 7.
As the boxes open
Energy transformation: nothing the body does is free, and that is why the sodium potassium pump exists. Communication and information flow is the whole back half of the course, endocrine and nervous. Then open the memory hook and say it twice.
Do this
Have them write control, compartments, energy, communication in the margin. They will use it as a checklist all term.
Slide 6 of 6

Hold onto this

Boxes on this slide The one line · What to do next

Open with
This is the close. Slow down.
As the boxes open
The one line is the takeaway. Then give them the working instruction: when a new topic starts, ask which theme it belongs to.
Do this
Assign the habit out loud. Every new topic, name the theme first, before taking a single note.

Close the deck with Tell them the eight themes are not a list to memorize. They are the questions you will keep asking all term.

P-02

Systems Theory and Physiology

slides-P-systems-theory.html · 4 slides

Before you start Short deck. The whole job is to make them stop studying organs in isolation.

Slide 2 of 4

A system is more than the sum of its parts

Open with
Ask what happens if you take a heart out of a body and keep it beating in a dish. Is it doing the same thing?
As the boxes open
Open the first box and let the phrase cannot be predicted do the work. The second box is why physiology is called integrative. The third box is the consequence they will be tested on: change one component and other components change.
Do this

Slide 3 of 4

As the boxes open
Let the silence run. When you take answers, take three. Then open blood loss, dehydration and altitude in that order. On each, trace the arrow out loud so they hear the chain rather than the endpoint.
Do this

Slide 4 of 4

Hold onto this

Open with
Close on the exam behavior, because this is the one that changes their scores.

Do this

P-03

Emergent Properties and Physiology

slides-P-emergent-properties.html · 5 slides

Before you start This is the most abstract deck of the week. The car analogy is the whole lesson, so protect the time for it.

Slide 2 of 5

Complex systems have emergent properties

Boxes on this slide The definition · Where they come from

Open with
Ask whether you could predict what water does from knowing hydrogen and oxygen separately.
As the boxes open
Open the definition. The phrase to sit on is cannot be predicted to exist. Then open the second box and make the source explicit: emergent properties come from interactions between parts, not from the parts themselves.
Do this
Write hydrogen plus oxygen equals wet on the board and let them argue with it.
Slide 3 of 5

The car analogy

Boxes on this slide Set it up · Now predict · Now scale it up

Open with
Walk the car analogy as a story, not a slide.
As the boxes open
Set it up with the parts on the floor. Ask the prediction question and wait, genuinely, before opening the second box. Then scale it up on the third box and let the jump from molecules to a thinking organism land without softening it.
Do this
Pause after the second box. If nobody answers, ask them to say yes or no by show of hands, then open it.
Slide 4 of 5

Emergent properties in humans

Boxes on this slide Emotions · Intelligence · Other aspects of brain function

Open with
Ask them to name a property of a person that no single cell has.
As the boxes open
Take answers before opening. Emotions and intelligence usually come up. Open them, then close on the line about nerve cells, and say it slowly: none of these can be predicted from the individual properties of nerve cells.
Do this
Take at least two student answers before you open a single box.
Slide 5 of 5

Hold onto this

Boxes on this slide The one line · Watch out for this

Open with
Close by heading off the wrong conclusion.
As the boxes open
The one line is straightforward. The watch out for this box is the important one, because bright students hear emergent and conclude unexplainable. Say directly that emergent means you study the interactions, and that this is what the rest of the course does.
Do this
Ask them where they expect to meet emergent properties later. Consciousness, blood pressure and body temperature are all fair answers.

Close the deck with Emergent does not mean mysterious. It means you have to look at the interactions, which is exactly what physiology does.

Approaches to Physiology

Before you start This deck is short but it pays off on every exam. Students lose points here for answering the wrong question, not for not knowing the material.

Slide 2 of 5

There are two ways to explain any physiological process

Boxes on this slide Teleological approach · Mechanistic approach

Open with
Ask why we breathe. Take an answer. Then ask how we breathe. Let them hear the difference.
As the boxes open
Open teleological and name it as the why. Open mechanistic and name it as the how. Do not let purpose sound like the lesser answer; both are real answers and both are expected of them.
Do this
Write why and how on the board and put the two student answers under them.
Slide 3 of 5

Same example, both answers

Open with
Use the oxygen example exactly as written, because they will see it again.
As the boxes open
State the process, then open the why, then the how. Say the pair out loud back to back so the contrast is audible. Cells need oxygen and red blood cells bring it. Oxygen binds to hemoglobin.
Do this
Ask them to do the same two answers for the stomach making acid before you move on.

Do this
Say the hook twice and have them write it down.
Slide 5 of 5

P-06

Homeostasis, the General Idea

slides-P-homeostasis.html · 6 slides

Before you start The single most important deck of the week. Everything after this is an application of it.

Slide 2 of 6

Break the word apart

Boxes on this slide The parts · The trap · A better word

Open with
Write homeostasis on the board and split it before you open anything.
As the boxes open
Homeo, similar. Stasis, condition. Then open the trap box and correct the word directly: stasis here does not mean static. Then give them homeodynamic and say that it is the better word, because small changes are always happening.
Do this
Ask for a show of hands: who thought homeostasis meant staying the same? Most hands go up. Use that.
Slide 3 of 6

What homeostasis actually is

Boxes on this slide The working definition · Picture it

Open with
Ask what the body is actually doing when it holds a value steady.
As the boxes open
Open the working definition and stress monitors and takes corrective action. Then open the picture box: not a locked thermostat, a hand on the wheel, correcting all the time.
Do this
Hold your hands on an imaginary wheel and make the small corrections. They remember the gesture.
Slide 4 of 6

Boxes on this slide The result · Pathophysiology · Why this matters clinically

Open with
Ask what happens when this fails. Do not answer it for them.
As the boxes open
Disease. Open it, then define pathophysiology as the study of body functions in a disease state. Then the third box, which is the sentence that reframes the whole course: almost every disease is a control loop that stopped holding.
Do this
Say the control loop line slowly. It is the thesis of your course.
Slide 5 of 6

Where disease comes from

Boxes on this slide Internal origin · External origin · Predict, then check

Open with
Two origins. Ask them to sort a few examples before you open anything.
As the boxes open
Internal first: cancer, autoimmunity, organ or cell failure. External second: toxins, trauma, infections, medications. Then run the predict and check box on a drug side effect, and make them commit to an answer before you open it.
Do this
Give them three conditions and have them call out internal or external before you reveal the categories.
Slide 6 of 6

Hold onto this

Boxes on this slide The one line · Carry it forward

Open with
Close on the two lines that carry forward.
As the boxes open
Homeostasis is active work, not a resting state. Then the carry forward instruction about the system units.
Do this
Have them write what is this system holding steady, and how on the inside cover of their notebook.

Close the deck with Tell them that every system unit later in the term is another answer to what is this system holding steady, and how.

P-08

Homeostasis and Mass Balance

slides-P-mass-balance.html · 6 slides

Before you start First quantitative deck. Some students tense up here, so say early that the arithmetic is addition and subtraction.

Slide 2 of 6

The body is an open system

Boxes on this slide What that means · The consequence · Load

Open with
Ask whether the body is a sealed system. It is not, and that is the whole slide.
As the boxes open
Open the open system box and name the exchange of heat and materials. Then the consequence: to hold homeostasis the body must hold mass balance. Then define load as the amount in the body right now.
Do this
None needed. Keep this one brisk, it is setup.
Slide 3 of 6

The law of mass balance

Boxes on this slide The statement · The equation · Read it out loud

Open with
Put the equation on the board before you open it, and build it with them.
As the boxes open
Open the statement first: gain must be offset by equal loss. Then the equation. Then read it in plain words, which is the box that makes it stick: what comes in, plus what you make, minus what you get rid of, minus what you use up.
Do this
Write the equation on the board while you say the plain version. Do not just show it.
Slide 4 of 6

The two ways a substance leaves the picture

Boxes on this slide Excretion · Metabolism · The difference that matters

Open with
Ask what the difference is between excreting something and metabolizing it. Most will not have separated these.
As the boxes open
Excretion, out of the body, urine, feces, lungs, skin. Metabolism, converted to something else, original molecule becomes a metabolite. Then the third box is the one that matters: excretion removes it from the body, metabolism removes it from the pool while the atoms stay inside you.
Do this
This distinction shows up on the renal and hepatic material later. Flag that out loud.
Slide 5 of 6

Worked example: water

Boxes on this slide The output side · The input side · The balance · It is not only water

Open with
Run water as the worked example.
As the boxes open
Output first, sweat and urine. Input second, and do not skip metabolic water production, because they always forget it exists. Then the balance box. Then the last box, which generalizes: oxygen, carbon dioxide, salts and hydrogen ions all obey the same law.
Do this
Ask what happens to body water on a hot day with no water bottle. Walk their answer through the equation.
Slide 6 of 6

Hold onto this

Boxes on this slide The one line · Use it clinically

Open with
Close on the clinical use, because it is genuinely useful to them.
As the boxes open
In equals out. Then the diagnostic habit: a rising value means intake up or removal down.
Do this
Give them a rising potassium and ask which side of the equation moved. Take both answers, both are legitimate.

Close the deck with Give them the clinical habit: if a value is rising, either intake went up or removal went down. It is always one of the two.

P-11

Homeostasis Does Not Equal Equilibrium

slides-P-homeostasis-not-equilibrium.html · 5 slides

Before you start Conceptually the hardest deck of the week. The payoff is Week 4, so say so.

Slide 2 of 5

Three words that get mixed up

Boxes on this slide Steady state · Dynamic steady state · Equilibrium

Open with
Write steady and equal on the board and ask whether they mean the same thing.
As the boxes open
Steady state, no net movement. Dynamic steady state, stable but constantly moving back and forth, and say clearly that this is the one the body uses. Equilibrium, compositions identical, and say clearly that this is not what the body does.
Do this
Three terms, three lines on the board. Make them copy all three before you move on.
Slide 3 of 5

Your cells live in disequilibrium

Boxes on this slide Look at two compartments · Inside the cell · Outside the cell · The point

Open with
Ask what is inside a cell versus outside. Many will know potassium and sodium already.
As the boxes open
Open the two compartments framing, then potassium inside, then sodium and chloride outside. Then the point box: different concentrations means not at equilibrium, and yet stable.
Do this
Draw the cell again with the ions on the correct sides. They will redraw this many times this term.
Slide 4 of 5

Why this costs energy

Boxes on this slide Predict first · The answer · The memory hook

Open with
Ask the predict question and wait: if these ions can leak, why does the difference not disappear?
As the boxes open
Take answers first. Then open the answer, active transport, and say plainly that it requires ATP. Then the memory hook, equilibrium is free and dead, steady state is expensive and alive.
Do this
The hook is the line they quote back to you at the end of the term. Say it well.
Slide 5 of 5

Hold onto this

Boxes on this slide The one line · Where this returns

Open with
Close and set up Week 4.
As the boxes open
Steady while deliberately unequal. Then name the forward link to the resting membrane potential.
Do this
Tell them the gradient they just drew is what Week 4 is built on.

Close the deck with This gradient is the reason a resting membrane potential exists, which is where Week 4 starts.
