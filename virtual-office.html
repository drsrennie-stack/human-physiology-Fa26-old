Getting help, and giving it

Dr. Rennie's Virtual Office.

The Virtual Office is a question board in Canvas, open all term. It is the front door for course questions, and it is public on purpose. In a fully online class the hallway conversation after lecture does not happen by accident, so we hold it here, where everyone can benefit from it.

Open the Virtual Office in Canvas →

Why your question here helps everyone

If you are confused about something, you are almost certainly not the only one. You are just the one willing to say it out loud. A question asked in the Virtual Office gets answered once and read by the whole class, and the best ones get folded into the course's answered questions page, where they keep helping students for terms to come.

Asking a good question in public is a contribution to this class, and I treat it as one.

Which door for which question

Three places to bring something, and they do different jobs. Picking the right one gets you a useful answer faster.

Public board

The Virtual Office

  • Anything about the course that is not private: content you are stuck on, what an assignment is asking for, where something lives, a page that will not load.
  • Answers help the whole class, and classmates are welcome to answer first.

Private

  • Private things never go on the public board. Send those to me directly.

Five moves

How to ask a question that gets a great answer

None of this is about being formal. It is about giving whoever answers enough to actually help you.

  1. 1
    Check whether it is already answered.

    The answered questions page and Hootie resolve most questions in under a minute. Then search the Virtual Office itself. Your question may already have a thread, and replying there beats starting a twin.

  2. 2
    Give it a real title.

    "Confused about the second color on the note sheet" gets found and answered. "Help" gets neither.

  3. 3
    Say what you tried.

    Where you looked, what you expected, and what happened instead. For a broken page, a screenshot and the page name are worth more than a paragraph of description.

  4. 4
    One question per post.

    Three questions bundled into one post tend to get one and a half answers. Split them and each one gets a real reply.

  5. 5
    Come back and close the loop.

    If a reply solved it, say so. The next student with your question needs to know which answer worked.

Answering each other

You are allowed, and encouraged, to answer classmates in the Virtual Office. Explaining something you have just learned is one of the strongest study moves there is, and it is a good sign you actually have it. I read the board, so anything that needs correcting gets corrected.

If a page does not work for you, tell me there and I will fix it.
