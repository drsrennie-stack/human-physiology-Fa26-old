Step 1

Check your device

This course runs on interactive pages you open in your browser: simulators, drawing canvases, recall cards, and lab tools. This check tells you whether your device is ready for them, and what to change if it is not.

  • BrowserNot checked

    Chrome, Edge, Firefox, and Safari all work. Internet Explorer does not.

  • Screen sizeNot checked

    Phones are fine for reading. The lab simulators and the drawing canvas want a laptop or a tablet.

  • Saved progressNot checked

    The study tools remember where you left off. That only works if your browser lets this site store data on your own machine. Nothing leaves your device and nothing is sent to me.

  • Video and audioNot checked

    Every lecture is a recorded video with captions. Your browser needs to be able to play it.

  • ConnectionNot checked

    Pages are small and load fast. Video is the only part that needs steady bandwidth.

  • Your display settingsNot checked

    If you have reduced motion, higher contrast, or larger text turned on, the course pages follow your setting instead of overriding it.

Step 3

What this class asks of you

BIOL 5 is a four unit lab course. Four units is about 216 hours of work for the semester. A normal term spreads those hours across 18 weeks. Ours runs 14 weeks, so the same 216 hours have to fit into fewer of them.

Lababout 8 hours
Lecture materialabout 3 hours
Studying on your ownabout 5 hours
Every weekabout 15 to 16 hours

That is the minimum for a passing grade. It is not the number for an A, and it is not an estimate of how long the work takes if you are struggling with it.

Taking physiology online does not reduce any of that. It is the same course, the same 216 hours, and the same standard as a section that meets in a room. What changes is that you schedule the hours yourself instead of a room and a clock scheduling them for you. There is no version of this course that takes less time, and there is no shortcut that substitutes for the hours.
