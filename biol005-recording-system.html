Instructor Reference · Recording Protocol

How a mission lecture gets recorded

The old Week 1 library was twenty short concept segments, one per idea. The mission model needs a different shape: fewer recordings, each one carrying a problem from open to resolved. This is the protocol for producing them.

Two recordings per mission 18 to 24 minutes each Loom or Studio

What changes, and why

The old shape

  • Twenty segments, one concept each, three to eight minutes.
  • Each one opened by naming the concept and closed by defining it.
  • Order was the textbook's order.
  • A student could watch any one alone and it still made sense, which sounds like a feature and is actually the problem: nothing depended on anything.

The mission shape

  • Two recordings per mission, each one a full arc.
  • Opens with a problem the student cannot yet solve, closes with them able to solve it and then having it broken again.
  • Order is set by what the problem needs next.
  • Segment two depends on segment one. That dependency is the point.

Keeping the mission never justifies cutting physiology. If the story would need you to skip a mechanism, the mechanism stays and the story bends around it.

The six beats, every time

This is the same reasoning frame the students use on their patient file, so recording in this order teaches the frame twice: once in the content, once in the shape of the video.

The three second rule

At every predict point, ask the question and then say nothing for three seconds before you reveal. On a recording that silence feels endless to you and normal to them, and it is the only thing standing between a prediction and a demonstration they watched.

Before you press record

  1. Open the deck and hit Reset boxes

    Every reveal box in the deck closes. If you skip this, half your predict points are already answered on screen and the recording is wasted.

  2. Go to Present full screen

    The browser goes full screen and the deck shows one slide per screen. Arrow keys move you. Nothing on screen says the word slide, so it does not look like a slideshow.

  3. Turn the pen layer on and park the toolbar

    Press W, then drag the toolbar by the grip to wherever it is not in your way. It stays there for every future recording. Press T to hide it entirely once you are set.

  4. Start the recorder, then wait two seconds before speaking

    Gives you a clean head to trim to, and it stops the first three words of every video from being clipped.

  5. Do not restart for small mistakes

    Say the correction out loud and keep going. Students trust a person who corrects herself more than one who never needs to, and a retake costs twenty minutes to fix four seconds.

After the recording

  1. Chapter it while it is fresh

    One chapter per beat, six per segment. Name them for what happens, not for the topic. Write "Blood glucose starts falling" rather than "Negative feedback."

  2. Request captions and actually read them

    Auto-captions mangle physiology vocabulary in predictable ways. Osmolarity, afferent and efferent, and every eponym will need fixing. Budget five minutes.

  3. Paste the id and the timestamps into the video page

    The chapter list drives the slide viewer beside it, so the timestamps have to be the real ones, not estimates.

  4. Save the pen boards you built

    The pen layer saves each board as a PNG. The drawing you built live is the one students try to reproduce on their note sheet, so it belongs in the notes page too.

Naming, so future you can find things

m01-a-maintain-control.html

  • m01 is the mission number, zero padded, so files sort correctly forever.
  • a or b is the segment within the mission.
  • The rest is the mission name in lower case with hyphens.
  • The slides, the notes, and the video page for one segment share the stem and differ only in the suffix: -slides, -notes, -video.

What you are recording first

The first two lectures, both in Unit 1, Keep the Human Alive

M1Maintain controlCh 1A body loses control of one variable. Which one kills first, and how would you know it was slipping?
M2Build the molecular toolkitCh 2Everything in Mission 1 was done by molecules. Which molecules, and what makes one able to do a job another cannot?

Why these two, in this order

Mission 1 has to be first because it sets the frame every later mission runs inside. Mission 2 has to be second because the toolkit is what makes Mission 1's mechanisms physical rather than abstract. A student who meets proteins before they have a reason to need them learns a list. A student who meets them right after watching a control loop fail learns a tool.
