# BIO 005 Practice Exam + Gap Finder. Bank spec

One tool, `practice-exam.html`, used all term. It draws from `bio005-exam-bank.js`, which is GENERATED by `tools/build_exam_bank.js` from two sources:

1. The existing recall card bank (`os/bio005-card-bank.js`, 4980 multiple choice cards, already tagged to competency and DOK). These become the multiple choice pool.
2. Authored item files in `cards/exam/week-NN.json`, one per week, holding the four item types the card bank does not have: multiple answer, true or false with correction, brain dump, and application.

Never hand edit `bio005-exam-bank.js`. Edit the JSON and rebuild.

## Tags on every item

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| id | unique string, `wNN-type-nn` for authored items, `c123` for card bank items |
| type | `mc`, `ma`, `tf`, `bd`, `app` |
| competencyId | exact id from bio005-competencies.js |
| week | taken from the competency record at build time, never from the id prefix |
| topic | the competency's `general` field, filled at build time |
| dok | 1, 2 or 3 |

The generator reads `week` from the competency record. Id prefixes such as `w7-` are historical labels and are ignored.

## Blueprint enforced on every generated exam

Type mix, per 30 items (scaled for 10, 20, 50):

| type | per 30 |
|---|---|
| Multiple choice (mc) | 14 |
| Multiple answer (ma) | 5 |
| True or false, then correct it (tf) | 4 |
| Brain dump with self check (bd) | 3 |
| Application or case (app) | 4 |

DOK mix, over the whole exam: 10 percent DOK 1, 45 percent DOK 2, 45 percent DOK 3. Brain dump and application items are always DOK 3. The DOK 1 and DOK 2 quotas are filled from mc, ma and tf. If a cell of the type by DOK grid is empty for the chosen weeks, the generator borrows from the same type at the nearest DOK, then from the same DOK in another type, so an exam always fills.

Weeks are drawn proportionally to their competency count, with a floor so a selected week is never skipped.

## Item shapes (authored JSON)

```json
{ "id":"w03-ma-01", "type":"ma", "competencyId":"w2-secondary-active-transport", "dok":2,
  "stem":"Which of the following are true of secondary active transport? Select all that apply.",
  "options":["...","...","...","...","..."],
  "correct":[0,2,4],
  "why":"One short paragraph on the physiology.",
  "whyNot":["one line per option: why it belongs or why it does not","...","...","...","..."] }

{ "id":"w03-tf-01", "type":"tf", "competencyId":"w2-osmolarity-tonicity", "dok":2,
  "statement":"Osmosis is the movement of solute across a membrane toward the side with more water.",
  "truth":false,
  "correction":"Osmosis is the net movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane toward the side with the higher solute concentration.",
  "why":"One short paragraph on the physiology and the common confusion." }

{ "id":"w03-bd-01", "type":"bd", "competencyId":"w2-osmosis-cell-volume", "dok":3,
  "prompt":"Without notes, explain what happens to a red blood cell dropped into pure water, and why.",
  "rubric":["water moves toward the higher solute concentration, into the cell",
            "the membrane is permeable to water but not to the cell's solutes",
            "cell volume rises",
            "the membrane cannot stretch far, so the cell lyses (hemolysis)",
            "the solution is hypotonic to the cell"] }

{ "id":"w03-app-01", "type":"app", "competencyId":"w1-compartment-shifts", "dok":3,
  "case":"A marathon runner finishes in the heat and has been drinking only water for the last two hours. Her plasma sodium is 125 mmol/L (normal 135 to 145).",
  "stem":"Which way is water moving between her compartments right now, and what does that do to her brain cells?",
  "options":["Into the intracellular compartment; brain cells swell",
             "Out of the intracellular compartment; brain cells shrink",
             "Water is not moving; plasma sodium does not affect cell volume",
             "Into the plasma only; interstitial fluid is unaffected"],
  "correct":0,
  "why":"...", "whyNot":["...","...","...","..."] }
```

Multiple choice from the card bank is converted at build time to:

```json
{ "id":"c16", "type":"mc", "competencyId":"...", "dok":3, "stem":"...", "options":[...], "correct":0, "why":"explanation text" }
```

## Counts to author per week

12 multiple answer, 10 true or false, 8 brain dump, 12 application. Spread across the week's competencies; every competency gets at least one authored item where the week has 42 or fewer competencies. Application and brain dump items sit on the DOK 3 competencies first.

DOK for authored items: ma items are DOK 2 (a few DOK 1 allowed when the competency is DOK 1), tf items DOK 1 or 2, bd and app always DOK 3.

## Writing rules for every item

- Physiology, not anatomy. A question that only asks where something is or what it is called does not belong here.
- Verify the physiology. Never key an answer that is only arguably right. No distractor may also be defensible under the wording of the stem.
- Student friendly language, short sentences, like a professor talking. No AI phrasing.
- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, periods or parentheses.
- No italics, no markdown, no HTML inside strings.
- Temperatures in Celsius with Fahrenheit in parentheses.
- For every wrong option, say what concept it represents or when it would be right, so the distractors teach.
- Every tf statement that is false must be false for a physiological reason, not a trick of wording. About 3 in 10 tf items should be true; for a true item the correction field holds the one sentence reason it is true.
- Brain dump rubrics list 4 to 7 things a complete answer must contain, each as a short checkable phrase.
- Application cases give the numbers the student needs (with the reference range beside any lab value) and ask the student to predict or explain a mechanism, never to name a diagnosis.
