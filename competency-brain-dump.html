The competency brain dump

You do this twice for every week of this course. Once before you have been taught anything, and once after. The gap between the two sheets is the part that is actually yours.

Two passes Blank paper, closed book One upload

What this is

Every week has a competency list. A competency is one thing you should be able to do by the end of that week, written as a job rather than a topic. Under each one are two prompts, and nearly all of them ask you to draw something and label it.

The brain dump is you attempting those prompts on paper with nothing open. Not reading them and thinking that you could. Writing and drawing them, badly if that is where you are, and finding out for certain.

The reason it is graded on effort rather than accuracy is that a first pass done honestly is supposed to be full of holes. If your first pass is perfect, you either already knew the material or you looked something up, and only one of those helps you.

Recognizing something on a page is not the same as being able to produce it. The only way to tell the two apart is to close the page and try.

What you need

  • Blank paper. Lined is fine. You will need room to draw, so do not crowd it.
  • Something to write with. Pen is better than pencil, because you should be able to see what you crossed out.
  • The competency list for the week, open on a screen or printed. That is the only thing you are allowed to have open.
  • A timer, if you want one. Most weeks this takes twenty to thirty minutes per pass.

What you do not need: the week page, the lecture recording, your notes, a search engine, or an AI assistant. All of those are closed for this. They are open for plenty of other things in this course, and this is not one of them.

The first pass, before you watch anything

Do this at the very start of the week, before the recording and before the reading.

  1. Read the competency list once, straight through

    Do not look anything up while you read it. You are getting the shape of the week, not learning it.

  2. Pick one prompt per competency

    Each competency has two. Take whichever one you want. There is no harder or easier one, they come at the same idea from different directions.

  3. Attempt every one of them on paper

    Draw what you can. Label what you can. Write the sentences you can. When you get to one where you have nothing, write down what you think it might be about, and then write "not yet". Do not skip it and leave a blank space, because a blank tells you nothing later.

  4. Put the sheet somewhere you will find it again

    Do not throw it out, do not correct it, and do not go back and fill anything in after you have watched the lecture. That sheet is the before picture and it stops being useful the moment you improve it.

This will feel bad, and that is the mechanism

Trying to answer something before you have been taught it makes the teaching land harder. You will sit through the lecture recognizing the exact places you got stuck, and those are the parts you will still have in December. Students who skip the first pass because it feels pointless are skipping the part that does the work.

The second pass, after the lecture and the reading

Do this after you have watched the mission recording and worked the week page, and before you sit the knowledge check.

  1. Fresh paper, and the first sheet stays face down

    Same rules as before. Competency list open, nothing else.

  2. Work the same prompts again

    Same one you picked the first time, so the two sheets compare directly. If you want the other one as well, do it underneath, not instead.

  3. Now put the two sheets side by side

    Anything that filled in between the first pass and the second is something you learned this week. Anything still thin or still blank is what you have not got yet, and it is not going to fix itself.

  4. Write your miss list at the bottom of the second sheet

    Just the competency numbers you still could not produce. That list is what you study before the exam, and it is far more useful than re-reading a page you have already read twice.

How to label your paper

You do not copy the prompt out. You write its label and then answer it. The label is the competency number, then a dash, then which of the two prompts you took.

3-1

Competency 3, first prompt.

3-2

Competency 3, second prompt.

On the competency list the first prompt is in a red box and the second is in a blue one, so you can find them by eye. Head each answer with its label and start a new line. That is the whole system.

What I am looking for

  • Drawings, not paragraphs. Most of these prompts ask for a diagram with labels on it. A written description of a diagram is not a diagram.
  • Labels on the drawing. An unlabelled sketch does not show me what you know. Label the parts, and label the arrows too, because in physiology the arrows are usually the point.
  • Your own handwriting on your own paper. Typed answers are not accepted for this. The whole point is that it comes out of your head and onto paper with nothing in between.
  • Honest effort. A real attempt earns full credit even when it is wrong. A few scattered words does not.

What does not count

Copying the prompt out and leaving it. Writing "I don't know" with nothing else on the whole page. Answering with the page open, which shows up immediately because those answers use the wording from the page and the real ones do not. And doing both passes in the same sitting, which defeats the entire design.

Turning it in

Photograph or scan both sheets and upload them together as one submission in Canvas. Phone photos are fine as long as the whole page is in frame and the writing is readable. If your drawings ran onto extra sheets, include those too.

Questions people ask

Can I do both passes on the same day?

No. The first pass has to happen before you have been taught the material and the second after, and there is no way to compress that into one sitting. If you are short on time, do the first pass in ten minutes rather than skipping it.

What if I genuinely cannot answer anything on the first pass?

That is a normal week one result and it is not a problem. Write what you think each prompt is asking about, mark it "not yet", and move on. Your second sheet is going to look very different and that difference is the whole point.

Do I have to do every competency?

Yes, one prompt from each. Some weeks that is seven competencies and some weeks it is twelve. The competency list for the week tells you how many.

Can I use the other prompt on the second pass?

You can add it, but do the one you did the first time as well. If the two sheets do not have the same prompts on them they do not compare, and the comparison is what you are being asked to produce.

What if my drawing is bad?

Nobody is grading the drawing as a drawing. A box with a word in it is a fine box. What matters is that the parts are there, they are labeled, and the arrows go the right way.
