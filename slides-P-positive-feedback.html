Positive Feedback Loops

The loop that makes the change bigger, and why something outside it has to stop it.

P-16 Physiology

Positive feedback is not homeostatic

Positive feedback loops are not homeostatic. They are not trying to hold a set point.

The response reinforces the stimulus rather than decreasing or removing it.

Farther from its normal value, increasing, sometimes temporarily out of control.

How does it ever stop

It requires something outside the loop, an intervention or an event, to stop the response.

Negative feedback shuts itself off. Positive feedback cannot.

Childbirth. Stretch of the cervix drives oxytocin release, oxytocin drives stronger contractions, contractions increase stretch. The delivery of the baby is the outside event that ends it.

Hold onto this

Positive feedback amplifies. It is useful exactly when the body needs a process finished quickly rather than held steady.

If the response makes the stimulus bigger and an outside event is needed to stop it, that is positive feedback.
