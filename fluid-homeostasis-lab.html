The fluid balance lab, and the lab test.

No Pearson this week. Your first lab is a patient, because Week 1's whole story, setpoints, mass balance, where the water is, walks through a hospital door every single day. You will work the case, then read the same physiology the way a clinician does, as two numbers on a blood panel.

Two parts, in order: work the fluid homeostasis case, then this sheet, by hand, and submit everything as one PDF in Canvas. There is nothing to complete in Pearson this week.

The five parts, in order

  1. Before you touch anything: predict

    In your first color, from what you already know. Do not look anything up; a wrong prediction is worth more than a looked-up one.

    1. You spend an afternoon working outside in the heat, sweating hard, drinking nothing. Predict what happens to the concentration of your body fluids, and say why.
    2. Your body loses water by several routes. Predict which routes it can shut down to nearly zero and which ones keep running no matter what.
    3. Rate your confidence in each prediction, 1 to 5. You will come back to these.

  2. Work the case

    Open the fluid homeostasis case and work it through, on paper, as the patient file asks. Open the Week 1 case →

  3. Draw the loop that failed

    1. Thirst is a control system. Draw its five components, sensor to response, for a body that is losing water, and mark the feedback arrow.
    2. Do a mass balance ledger for your case patient: every route water came in, every route it went out, and circle the route that kept running when intake stopped.

  4. The clinical correlation: read the lab test

    Fluid balance has a blood test. When the body loses water and does not replace it, everything dissolved in the plasma gets more concentrated, and two numbers rise together.

    The patient. A 78 year old is brought in from home during a heat wave: confused, dry mouth, almost no urine all day. Her family says she "drinks when she is thirsty." The panel:

    TestPatientReference range
    Serum sodium156 mmol/L136 to 145 mmol/L
    Serum osmolality322 mOsm/kg275 to 295 mOsm/kg
    1. Nothing added sodium to this patient. Explain how both numbers rose anyway, using mass balance and nothing else.
    2. Which of the five loop components most likely failed in this patient, and what is your evidence from the story? Aging quietly turns down one of them.
    3. Her cells have been sitting in concentrated fluid all day. Which way has water been moving across their membranes, and what does that do to the cells doing the thinking?
    4. Treatment is water, given slowly on purpose. Predict what each number does as water goes in, and offer one sentence on why racing the correction might be dangerous for the same cells.

  5. Look back at your thinking

    1. Return to your predictions. Right, wrong, and for each wrong one: what were you assuming?
    2. In your second color, the two or three things this lab gave you that you did not have walking in. That is your commit to memory list.
    3. Re-rate your confidence, 1 to 5, on explaining this case to a classmate. If it did not move, bring the sticking point to office hours or the discussion.
