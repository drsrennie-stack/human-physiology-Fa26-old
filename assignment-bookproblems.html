Practice · no points · two ways to use it

Book problems

These carry no points and nothing here is submitted. They are here because they are the closest thing in the course to what the exam asks you to do, and because working them tells you something a reread cannot.

Two ways to use these, and both count as learning

Pick the one that matches where you are with the material. If you have no idea where to start on a problem, the first route will just teach you that you are stuck, which you already knew. The second route exists for exactly that situation, and it is not a lesser option.

Route 1

Work them forwards

Use this when you have a way in, even a shaky one.

  1. By hand, before you look anything up. Get as far as you can from what you already know. The reasoning is the point, not the answer.
  2. Write down where each piece came from as you go: the page, the figure, the video.
  3. Then check the answer. Not before. A problem you watched yourself solve is not a problem you can solve.
  4. Mark the exact step where you went wrong, not just that you did. That step is what to take back to the Mastery Check.

Route 2

Work them backwards

Use this when you open a problem and have genuinely no idea how to begin.

  1. Read the worked solution first, slowly. You are not cheating. You are studying an example of the reasoning before trying to produce it.
  2. Beside each line, write why that step was taken. Not what it says, why it is there. This is the part that does the work, and skipping it turns the whole thing back into reading.
  3. Close it and reproduce the solution from blank paper. If you cannot, open it again, find the step you lost, and close it again.
  4. Then do the next problem of the same type cold. That is the test of whether the example transferred. If it did not, you have learned something useful and cheaply.

Route 2 is not the easy option. Done properly it takes longer than Route 1, because writing why each step exists is harder than following along. What it does is get you moving on a problem you would otherwise have stared at for twenty minutes and abandoned. Most students should be using Route 2 early in a topic and Route 1 later in the same topic.
