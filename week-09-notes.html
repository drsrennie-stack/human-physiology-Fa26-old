What this week covers

The heart as a pump. Work through these in order. Each line is one thing you will be able to do by Sunday.

  1. Adrenal medulla. Explain why the adrenal medulla is a modified sympathetic ganglion and compare the duration of its circulating catecholamine effect with direct sympathetic innervation.
  2. Pacemaker potential. Explain the unstable pacemaker potential of the sinoatrial node by the funny current, calcium entry, and potassium exit and state what sets intrinsic heart rate.
  3. Contractile cell action potential. Diagram the ventricular action potential and identify the ion movement responsible for the rapid upstroke, the plateau, and repolarization.
  4. Conduction system. Trace an impulse through the sinoatrial node, internodal pathways, atrioventricular node, bundle of His, bundle branches, and Purkinje fibers and explain the purpose of the atrioventricular delay.
  5. Cardiac refractory period. Explain why the long refractory period of cardiac muscle prevents tetanus and why that matters for pump function.
  6. ECG waves and intervals. Label the P wave, QRS complex, T wave, PR interval, and QT interval and state the electrical event each represents.
  7. ECG interpretation. Calculate heart rate from an ECG strip and identify sinus rhythm, tachycardia, bradycardia, and a first degree or complete heart block.
  8. ECG recording. Record a lead II ECG, measure the intervals, and correlate each wave with the mechanical event that follows it.
  9. Cardiac cycle. Order the phases of the cardiac cycle and state the pressure relationship that opens and closes the atrioventricular and semilunar valves in each phase.
  10. Pressure volume loop. Label the four phases of a ventricular pressure volume loop and predict how the loop changes with increased preload, increased afterload, or increased contractility.
  11. Heart sounds. Explain the origin of the first and second heart sounds and relate a murmur to valve stenosis or regurgitation by its timing.
  12. Stroke volume and ejection fraction. Calculate stroke volume and ejection fraction from end diastolic and end systolic volumes and interpret a reduced ejection fraction.
  13. Cardiac output. Calculate cardiac output and cardiac reserve and predict the effect of a change in heart rate or stroke volume on each.
  14. Frank Starling relationship. State the Frank Starling law and explain how venous return and end diastolic volume determine the force of the next contraction.
  15. Preload afterload and contractility. Distinguish preload, afterload, and contractility and predict how a change in each alters stroke volume.
  16. Autonomic regulation of the heart. Explain how sympathetic and parasympathetic input alter heart rate and contractility at the receptor and second messenger level.
  17. Heart sound and pulse correlation. Auscultate the heart sounds, locate the valve areas, and correlate the sounds with the pulse and with the cardiac cycle phases.
