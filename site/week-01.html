What to watch for 4 things

Week 1 is short, Tuesday to Sunday, so it is deliberately light on content and heavy on getting set up. Do the setup first. It takes an hour and it saves you from finding out in Week 6 that something does not work.

  • The difference between homeostasis and equilibrium. They are not the same thing and the distinction shows up on every exam in this course.
  • The seven parts of a feedback loop. Learn them once here and you will recognize them in every system for the rest of the term.
  • Osmolarity counts particles, not molecules. This one sentence will save you in Week 3.
  • A number without a unit is not an answer. Start that habit now.
Structures you should already know 4

Week 1 is mostly concepts rather than structures, so this list is short. It gets longer from Week 3 on.

  • Cell membrane
  • Nucleus, mitochondria, ribosome
  • The four tissue types
  • Where the extracellular fluid is

Not familiar with one? The anatomy review page covers them, and OpenStax is free and searchable. You do not need to have taken anatomy to do well here. You do need to look these up when they come past.

Competencies this week covers 14

Foundations of Physiology, Quantitative Skills for Physiology. Nothing on an exam comes from outside this list.

  • w1-levels-function Physiology and levels of functionDefine physiology and place a given process at the correct level of organization from molecule to organism, then state the level at which that process is best explained.
  • w1-structure-function Structure and function relationshipPredict how a change in the structure of a molecule, cell, tissue, or organ alters its function, using examples from two different organ systems.
  • w1-homeostasis Homeostasis definedDefine homeostasis, regulated variable, and setpoint, and distinguish homeostasis from chemical equilibrium and from steady state.
  • w1-feedback-components Feedback loop componentsDiagram a negative feedback loop labeling stimulus, sensor, afferent path, integrating center, efferent path, effector, and response, and trace body temperature or blood glucose through every step.
  • w1-feedback-types Negative and positive feedbackDistinguish negative from positive feedback by the direction of the response and give a physiological example of each, explaining why a positive feedback loop needs an outside event to end it.
  • w1-feedforward Feedforward control and acclimatizationExplain anticipatory feedforward control and acclimatization and identify which one is operating in a given scenario.
  • w1-control-pathways Local and reflex control pathwaysDistinguish a local control pathway from a long distance reflex pathway and classify a given response as one or the other.
  • w1-mass-balance Mass balanceApply the mass balance equation to a solute or to body water and calculate the intake, production, and output combination that holds the amount in the body constant.
  • w1-fluid-compartments Body fluid compartmentsState the approximate volumes of total body water, intracellular fluid, extracellular fluid, plasma, and interstitial fluid in a 70 kg adult and compare the dominant solutes of the intracellular and extracellular compartments.
  • w1-compartment-shifts Compartment separation and clinical volume shiftsPredict the direction of water movement between compartments when extracellular osmolarity rises or falls and name a clinical situation that produces each shift.
  • w1-units-conversion Units and unit conversionConvert among the units used in physiology including molarity, osmolarity, milliequivalents, mmHg, liters per minute, and percent solutions.
  • w1-lab-graphing Graphing and data interpretationConstruct a labeled graph with the independent variable on the x axis, and read slope, direction, and trend from a physiological data set.
  • w1-lab-experimental-design Experimental design and controlsIdentify the hypothesis, independent variable, dependent variable, and control condition in a physiology experiment and state what the control rules out.
  • w1-lab-measurement-error Measurement error and variabilityDistinguish random from systematic error and explain why physiological measurements are repeated and averaged.
